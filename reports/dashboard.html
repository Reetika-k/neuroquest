7. Fixed versus adaptive difficulty

The same simulated players replay the same seeded mazes under both conditions (40 per cell). With this many episodes the noise on a win-rate difference is roughly ±20 points, so small differences are not evidence of improvement; the experiment shows the controller is safe and traceable.

8. Reinforcement learning agent

A tabular Q-learning agent plays the game's own rules on one fixed 11x9 board: 43 open cells, 4 crystals, 1 astar hunter moving every 2 ticks, 200 ticks on the clock. It observes only its own cell, which crystals remain and where the hunter is (exactly within ±2 cells, otherwise only the direction). It never sees the clock, its health or the walls.

Reward per tick: -0.01 time cost · +1 per crystal · +5 at the exit · -2 per hit · -5 more when health reaches zero. Episode ends at the exit or at zero health; running out of time is a cut-off the learner bootstraps through. Training: 20000 ε-greedy episodes, α = 0.2, γ = 0.95, ε 1.0 → 0.05 over the first 80%; 1753 observations visited (bound 23392); 12 s.

9. Next-move model

A second supervised model predicts the player's next action (up, down, left, right, stay) from what the game shows at that tick: the last three actions, open and visited neighbours, the direction and distance of the nearest crystal, the portal and the nearest hunter, and the shortest-path direction to the nearest crystal and to the portal. Rows come from replaying 360 simulated episodes through the real rules (86726 ticks). Temporal split: episodes are ordered by seed; seeds 0–95 train (69976 rows), seeds 96–119 test (16750 rows); the model was chosen on the last 15206 training rows, never on the test rows.

10. Limitations

  • The classifier recognises simulated personalities; human play may differ. Human rows are kept separate so the gap can be measured as they accumulate.
  • Early in a quiet episode an explorer and an aggressive player look alike (no enemy met yet); the model is confidently wrong there, and its probabilities are not calibrated.
  • One split seed and 360 episodes: quote scores with the ±0.015 CV spread in mind.
  • The adaptive controller is rule-based with reasoned, untuned thresholds; its effect on humans is untested, and on simulated players it is safe but not shown to improve outcomes.
  • The Q-learning agent learns one small board with one hunter; it sees a partial observation and its table does not transfer to other boards. Its scores are a comparison on that board, not a claim about the full game.
  • The next-move model is trained and tested on simulated players whose decisions follow written rules; its accuracy on human play is unknown, and the hand-written greedy rule is a strong competitor for a reason.
